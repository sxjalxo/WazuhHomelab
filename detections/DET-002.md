Status labels are defined in the [README](../README.md#evidence-status-legend).

All tests are **controlled repeated authentication failure tests** run by the operator from the Kali VM, each attempt a single wrong password for the non-existent account `invaliduser`. They are not brute force or password spraying. The point is to measure *when* Wazuh correlates repeated failures, and when it stops.

## 1. Problem statement

Every failed SSH password already raises per-event alerts:
- **5710:** "Invalid user" and "Failed password for invalid user" lines.
- **5503:** the PAM failure.
- **5760:** failures against real accounts.

One wrong password is routine. Many failures from one source are not. The roadmap called for a correlation rule keyed on `srcip`, which [DET-001](DET-001.md) and [DET-003](DET-003.md) made possible across all SSH failure alerts.

Before writing anything, the question was **what the built-in ruleset already correlates, and where it stops**. A custom rule that duplicates a working built-in rule adds noise, not coverage.

## 2. Built-in correlation rules (Verified, E-43)

| Rule | Counts | Threshold | Same source IP | Level | MITRE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5712 | 5710 (non-existent user) | 8 in 120 s, then silent 60 s | yes | 10 | T1110 |
| 5763 | 5760 (failed password, real user) | 8 in 120 s, then silent 60 s | yes | 10 | T1110 |
| 5720 | 5716 (sshd authentication failed) | 8, no timeframe given | yes | 10 | T1110 |
| 5551 | 5503 (PAM failure) | 8 in 180 s | yes | 10 | T1110 |
| 40111 | any event in group `authentication_failed` | 12 in 160 s | yes | 10 | T1110 |

All of these are **fast-burst** rules, with windows of two to three minutes.

## 3. Baseline: fast burst (Verified, E-41, E-42, E-44)

Kali opened 9 single-attempt connections (the first loop pass was spent installing `sshpass`). The endpoint logged them between 14:47:21.885 and 14:48:04.067 UTC: 42.2 s, about one connection every 5 s.

Each connection logged five lines:
- `Invalid user`
- `check pass; user unknown`
- `pam_unix … authentication failure`
- `Failed password`
- `Connection closed`

That gives **2 × rule 5710 and 1 × rule 5503 per connection**. One attempt per connection means no PAM summary line, so 2502 and 100100 do not appear.

| Rule | Fired | Time (UTC) | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5712 | **yes** | 14:47:39.500 | 18 s, on the **8th** 5710 event (`previous_output` = 7 + current) |
| 5551 | **yes** | 14:47:57.519 | 36 s, on the **8th** 5503 event |
| 40111 | **no** | — | 26 `authentication_failed` events in 42 s |

All 27 alerts are accounted for:
- **5710:** 18 events, giving 17 alerts plus the one that became 5712.
- **5503:** 9 events, giving 8 alerts plus the one that became 5551.

Two behaviours confirmed here matter for the design:
- **When `frequency="8"` fires.** It fires on the 8th matching event, with no off-by-one.
- **The triggering event changes rules.** It is reported as the correlation rule instead of its parent rule, so it does not increment the parent's `firedtimes`.

One 5710 alert (14:47:59.518) reports `firedtimes: 2` in the middle of a run that otherwise counts to 16. The cause was not investigated.

## 4. Rule 40111 does not fire here (Verified, E-45)

40111 is the only built-in rule that correlates *mixed* failures, such as 5710 together with 5503. It did not fire in the burst, and `alerts.json` held **0** alerts from it for the whole day.

A controlled test in `wazuh-logtest` reproduced the miss:
- **Input:** 12 lines, alternating 5710 and 5503 (6 each) from one source IP, piped into **one** session. `firedtimes` counting up 1→6 confirms the session kept its state.
- **Design:** neither single-rule correlation rule could reach 8 matches, so only 40111 could fire, on the 12th line.
- **Result:** it did not fire.

The manager's `<ruleset>` excludes only `0215-policy_rules.xml`, so 40111's file is loaded. **Why it does not fire is not established.** For DET-002 this has a practical consequence: the design avoids `if_matched_group` and uses `if_matched_sid`, the mechanism that 5712 and 5551 just showed working live.

An earlier attempt pasted each line into a new logtest session, which resets correlation state. That run was invalid and is not used.

## 5. The gap: slow pace (Verified, E-46, E-47, E-48)

The same single-attempt connections were repeated with a 75 s pause between them. The endpoint logged 10 connections from 15:10:19 to 15:22:04 UTC, starting about every 78 s.

The alerts also show **three earlier Kali connections** at the same rhythm (15:07:19, 15:08:37, 15:09:55), outside the endpoint capture window. They are the start of an earlier run of the same loop that the operator stopped with Ctrl+C (**Reported**). Counting them, the window holds 13 failing connections from one source over about 15 minutes.

**Result:** 26 × 5710 and 13 × 5503 alerts, all at level 5. **No 5712, 5551 or 40111.**

The thresholds explain it. In this test each connection produced 2 × 5710 and 1 × 5503:

| Rule | Needs | Slowest pace that still fires |
|---|---|---|
| 5712 | 8 × 5710 in 120 s = 4 connections | about one connection every 40 s |
| 5551 | 8 × 5503 in 180 s = 8 connections | about one connection every 26 s |
| 40111 | (not firing at all here, §4) | — |

**Once failing connections are more than about 40 s apart, no built-in rule correlates them.** One wrong password per minute from the same IP is indistinguishable from scattered typos. Over an hour that is 60 guesses with no correlation alert.

## 6. Rule implementation

```xml
<group name="local,syslog,pam,">
  <rule id="100200" level="10" frequency="5" timeframe="1800" ignore="600">
    <if_matched_sid>5503</if_matched_sid>
    <same_source_ip />
    <description>PAM: repeated failed logins from one source over 30 min (low-rate guessing)</description>
    <mitre>
      <id>T1110.001</id>
    </mitre>
    <group>authentication_failures,</group>
  </rule>
</group>
```

| Choice | Reason |
|---|---|
| Counts **5503** | PAM logs `authentication failure` **once per failing connection**, whether the client makes one attempt or three. This holds in INC-001, DET-001 and every session in this document. So 5503 measures failing *connections*, and is not inflated by retries. It also fires for both account types: the real-account `pam_unix` line reaches 5503 too ([DET-001](DET-001.md) E-36 §4). 5710 counts only non-existent accounts, and counts two or more lines per connection. |
| `if_matched_sid` + `same_source_ip` | The mechanism 5712 and 5551 showed working live (§3). Avoids the `if_matched_group` path that 40111 shows not working here (§4). |
| 5 within 1800 s | Covers paces from 5551's limit (8 in 3 min) down to one failing connection every 7.5 min. Five failed SSH logins from one source in 30 minutes is unusual for a single legitimate user. |
| `ignore="600"` | At most one alert per 10 minutes, so a long slow run does not flood the queue. |
| Level 10 | Same as the built-in burst rules. This is the same behaviour at a slower rate, not a lesser one. |
| MITRE T1110.001 | Rule metadata naming the technique the pattern *could* indicate. It is not proof that password guessing took place. |
| Group `authentication_failures` | Matches the built-in correlation rules, so the alert appears in the same queries and dashboards. |

The deployed file matches this, with an explanatory comment inside the group (E-49 §4).

## 7. Tests in `wazuh-logtest` (Verified, E-49)

`wazuh-analysisd -t` at 15:43:11 reported only its usual INFO line.

The test input was 8 copies of the real `pam_unix … authentication failure` line from one source IP, piped into one session:

| Line | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | 5503 (`firedtimes` 1–4) | counting |
| **5** | **100200, level 10** | fires on the 5th, as configured |
| 6–7 | 5503 | `ignore="600"` suppresses repeats |
| 8 | 5503 (`firedtimes` 7) | **built-in 5551 did not fire** |

Line 8 shows how the new rule **interacts with the built-in rule**. The 5th event became 100200 instead of 5503, so 5503 had only 7 matches by line 8 and 5551 (8 × 5503) did not trigger. In a fast burst, 5551 is therefore expected to fire one failure later, on the 9th instead of the 8th. That is untested (§11).

## 8. Live validation (Verified, E-50, E-51, E-52)

The manager was restarted after the config test, and `wazuh-remoted` (pid 5669) was listening on 1514. Kali then made 6 single-attempt connections, 75 s apart. The endpoint logged them from 15:44:27.866 to 15:51:00.058 UTC.

It was **not** counted: `previous_output` lists only this run's failures. The rule did not exist before the restart at about 15:43, and analysisd keeps correlation state in memory. Either way, a manager restart starts the window again (§10).

## 9. Coverage before and after

| Pace from one source | Before DET-002 | With DET-002 |
|---|---|---|
| Faster than ~1 failing connection per 26 s | 5712 and 5551 | unchanged. 5551 expected one failure later (§7) |
| ~26–40 s | 5712 only | 5712, plus 100200 if 5 failures fall within 30 min |
| ~40 s to 7.5 min | **nothing** (proven at ~78 s, §5) | **100200** (proven at ~78 s, §8) |
| Slower than 7.5 min | nothing | nothing (§10) |

## 10. Limitations and validation scope

**Evasion:**
- **Slower attacks.** More than 7.5 minutes between failing connections, so that fewer than 5 fall within 30 minutes, still evades every rule.
- **One source IP only.** Guessing spread over many IPs (distributed attacks) is not correlated.
- **PAM failures only.** Rule 5503 needs PAM. Failures that never reach PAM, such as public-key-only attempts or `UsePAM no`, are not counted.

**State and scope:**
- **In-memory state.** Correlation is in memory, so a manager restart starts the 30-minute window again. Observed in §8.
- **`ignore` scope.** `ignore="600"` is expected to suppress the rule as a whole, not per source IP. If so, a second source in the same 10 minutes would get no alert of its own. This is untested.
- **Real-account path not run live.** Real-account failures reach 5503 (DET-001 E-36), but the live test used only the non-existent account.

**Side effects and tuning:**
- **5551 shift.** The new rule takes one 5503 occurrence. In logtest, 5551 did not fire on the 8th failure. The 9th is expected but not tested.
- **False positives.** A legitimate user who fails five SSH logins in 30 minutes, or a script with a stale password, will trigger it. The threshold has not been tuned against real baseline traffic, of which this lab has none.

**Not explained:**
- Why 40111 does not fire (§4).
- The out-of-sequence `firedtimes: 2` (§3).

## 11. Follow-ups

- Pipe 10 PAM failure lines through logtest to confirm that 5551 then fires on the 9th line.
- Investigate 40111 separately. If it is fixed, mixed-failure correlation becomes available.
- Consider a distributed-source rule (failures against one host from many IPs), and active response for 100200 (roadmap Phase 4).
